# Lessons from the student's feedback

Each lesson fixed a draft the student rejected. Add new ones at the end, with the unit they came from.

## IT241 Module 10 and Module 11 (November 2026)

1. **A prose summary in slide order failed** ("module 10 summary sucked"). A summary is reorganized for revising, not shortened slides.
2. **Organize by topic, not by question type.** The first Module 11 sheet grouped everything by exam pattern (all term questions, then all rules…). It read badly. One topic at a time, with the same blocks inside each, worked.
3. **Term first, then meaning.** Questions that showed the definition and hid the term ("weird, the concept is above the thing") read backwards.
4. **Have Gemini write and organize it** ("use gemini in browser to improve organization and writing and ease of reading for human student"). Your job is the facts and the review.
5. **Only the exam's formats.** "No true or false should be in this page because our exam only MCQ and open questions." That includes yes/no questions.
6. **Fix-only rounds with Gemini.** "Try not to ask for full sheet, you can stitch it together yourself." Full rewrites were slow and dropped content.
7. **Colour markers like a student's notebook** help focus ("use color markers like notebook for student to focus on words or important concepts").
8. **But calm:** lower opacity, no stroke under the text, no Test me mode, no sticky header, a wider column, more vertical space.
9. **Pictures help memory** ("it easy to memorise visual stuff"). Add them, and remove nothing.
10. **Read-along should be smooth and help understanding:** a pause after questions, pairs read as pairs.
11. **Check for exam-worthy concepts past papers never asked** ("consider if the exam tests new things not in past exams but they are core concepts"). A coverage check against the slides answers it.
12. **Pure black background** was preferred over navy.
13. **No empty blocks.** Removing duplicates left an empty Traps box, and the student noticed it.
14. **Work step by step.** Each step gets full attention before the next ("not rush it all at once").
